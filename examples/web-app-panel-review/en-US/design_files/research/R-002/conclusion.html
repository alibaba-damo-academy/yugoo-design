Explicit commitment and deferral

Confirmation, decline, return and deferral have different meanings. Confirmation establishes a commitment; decline rejects the addition; return identifies material that can be supplied; deferral states a condition for reassessment. BR-005 therefore requires the outstanding decision rather than an invented date to complete a form.

In PG-003, the lead chooses an outcome beside the agreement and assessment. O-005 records the decision, while O-003 retains the feedback’s handling status. They describe different stages of one journey; a status label is not another commitment. After the attachment and cost evidence arrive, reassess in the original record while retaining the agreed main page.

Notification failure and retry

The O-005 decision and O-006 delivery outcome are separate facts. Failed delivery does not cancel the decision, and an unknown receipt is not a definite failure. The lead first checks the decision, recipient and channel to avoid resubmitting a decision or notifying someone who already received it.

BR-006, P-002 and N-005 describe a reviewed retry of the same delivery. PG-004 shows decision and delivery together, with failure context and a next action; Q-012 lists the facts to check. No automatic retry count, waiting period or provider guarantee is defined. I-002 remains an unconnected design declaration and needs separate service and recovery testing after implementation.

Adoption boundaries for AI drafts

A-001 drafts from authorized current-customer material with record references rather than making commitments. A-002 can review risks, but its response remains an explanation for human review. Unreadable material must remain an explicit gap; model completion cannot become the customer’s words or the agreement.

Skill bodies, tool purposes and resource declarations explain evidence checks, access and recovery; none is installed or executed by this example.
