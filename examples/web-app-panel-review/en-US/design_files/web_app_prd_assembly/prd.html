Loopline Delivery: feedback and commitment requirements

Product requirements document

Document status

A manually prepared synthetic design for reviewing all Panels. Acceptance items are criteria for a proposed product, not passed test records. There is no real customer data, model call or sent notification.

Product definition

A six-person design studio receives additional customer changes before a Friday delivery. Feedback is spread across email, chat and review notes. Designers need to retain the customer’s words and compare them with the agreement; the lead needs evidence before deciding whether to make another commitment.

Loopline Delivery connects feedback intake, impact assessment, explicit decisions and notification delivery through traceable records. It helps people understand boundaries without confusing an AI suggestion, a customer’s request or delivered email with a lead commitment.

The automatic commitment proposal F-008 is rejected. Contract signing, automatic pricing and cross-customer data aggregation are outside scope. Existing human explanations remain intact, while retry windows and retention periods remain for the studio to confirm.

Users, scenarios and scope

Customers provide original feedback, designers assess impact, leads confirm additional commitments, and administrators manage membership and connections. Roles come from explicit grants rather than names, email domains or AI inference. The first scope prioritizes the complete F-001–004 feedback journey and also presents follow-up, search and drafting assistance.

Product plan and functional requirements

Group capabilities by the user’s task: connect original feedback and scope evidence, then route commitments to the lead. High priority does not mean automatic execution; formal decisions always retain human responsibility.

F-001 · Capture and link feedback

Keep the customer’s words, attachments and delivery item together without turning an unclear request into a new commitment.

Before a Friday delivery, a customer asks to revise an agreed design. The six-person studio must retain the customer’s words and distinguish a small change, additional scope and missing information. Business rules and controls

These rules prevent unclear feedback from becoming an unauthorized commitment and distinguish missing information, deferral, failure and concurrency. Each rule states its condition and deterministic outcome.

IDRuleConditionOutcome
BR-001Request missing information firstThe feedback cannot be linked to a delivery item or does not describe the desired change.Retain feedback and attachments, identify a concrete gap, and do not infer effort.
BR-002Keep evidence for small changesThe change adds no scope and changes no delivery milestone, and the designer has recorded the basis.The designer may handle it directly, retaining the assessment and outcome for the lead.
BR-003A lead confirms new commitmentsThe request adds scope or cost, or affects a delivery milestone.Request lead confirmation; do not communicate a committed date until an explicit decision is saved.
BR-004Return is different from declineThe lead needs more evidence before deciding.Retain the request, explain what is missing, and allow revision and resubmission.
BR-005Explain what a deferral awaitsThe lead chooses to defer the request.State the outstanding business decision and follow-up condition; do not present a deferral as a decline.
BR-006Separate saving from deliveryA decision is saved but the external notification service has not confirmed delivery.Keep the decision valid, show failed delivery and allow retry without duplicating the decision.
BR-007Explicitly adopt an AI draftThe assistant returns an impact explanation that has not been adopted.Show an editable draft only; do not overwrite human explanations or change the decision status.
BR-008Review concurrent updatesAnother member saves a newer version of the same decision during review.Retain current input, show the conflict and latest content, and allow retry after review.
